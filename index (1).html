Physics behind the simulation

Gravitational potential energyPE = mgL(1 − cos θ)
Kinetic energyKE = ½mv² = ½mL²(dθ/dt)²
Conservation of mechanical energyKE + PE = mgL(1 − cos θ₀)

The pendulum is released from rest at its maximum angular displacement. At each extreme, its speed is zero, so PE is maximum and KE is zero. At equilibrium (the lowest point), KE is maximum and PE is zero. Between them, energy continuously changes from one form to the other.

The simulator integrates the nonlinear pendulum equation, d²θ/dt² + (g/L)sin θ = 0, using a fourth-order Runge–Kutta numerical method. Energy is calculated from the exact pendulum height and bob speed. This remains accurate for larger release angles, where the usual small-angle SHM approximation is less accurate.

Classroom discussion: Is the kinetic energy at half the maximum angular displacement equal to half the total energy? Why or why not? (Hint: potential energy depends on 1 − cos θ, not simply on θ.)
